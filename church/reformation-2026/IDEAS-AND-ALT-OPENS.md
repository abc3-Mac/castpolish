# Alternate Openings, Hooks, and Idea Bank

The main script opens with the 1505 thunderstorm. Here are four other cold opens that drop into the same 0:00–0:10 slot, plus hook lines, "why it matters today" angles, and extra ideas for follow-up posts.

## Alternate cold opens (swap into 0:00–0:10)

### A. "Here I Stand" — Diet of Worms, April 1521
Visual: torch-lit hall, a lone figure before a wall of robes and crowns. Slow dolly in.
*SFX: murmuring crowd falls silent. A single chair scrapes.*
VO: "April 1521. An emperor, princes, and the whole weight of Europe tell one monk: take it back. He answers: 'I cannot and will not recant. God help me.'"
(Note: "Here I stand, I can do no other" is the famous version but may be a later addition; the line above is the well-attested one.)

### B. The Hammer — Wittenberg door, October 31, 1517
Visual: pre-dawn, black screen, three hammer strikes, each one a hard white flash revealing more of YOUR Wittenberg door photo. Caption: "October 31, 1517."
VO: "Three nails. One sheet of paper. Ninety-five sentences. Nobody in Wittenberg that morning knew the world had just changed."
(Then jump back to 1505 or go straight to the Romans beat.)

### C. The Post That Went Viral
Visual: printing press plates slam; sheets multiply across the screen like a social feed scrolling (Grok can do this well).
VO: "In 1517 a professor posted ninety-five sentences on a door. Within weeks they were in every major city in Germany. No internet. No phone. Just a press, and a message people were starving for."
Great for a younger audience; very "Shorts"-native.

### D. The Hidden Knight — Wartburg Castle, 1521–22
Visual: snow, a castle on a crag, a man with a beard in a cold room writing by candle.
VO: "Kidnapped by his friends. Hidden in a castle under a fake name. In eleven weeks, 'Knight George' gave the German people the New Testament in their own words."
Warm, less dramatic than the storm, strong tie-in to "read the Bible yourself."

## Hook lines for the first 2 seconds (caption on screen)

Shorts live or die in the first two seconds. Pick one caption to burn in over the opening frame:
- "One lightning bolt. Five hundred years."
- "He thought he could never be good enough."
- "The scariest night of Martin Luther's life"
- "Why do Lutherans celebrate October 31?"
- "Am I enough? A 500-year-old answer."

## "Why it still matters today" — practical angles for the pastor segment

Use one or two, not all. The script uses #1.
1. **Am I enough?** Performance culture, burnout, the quiet fear of not measuring up. The gospel says you don't have to be; Christ is.
2. **A clean conscience.** Luther confessed for hours and still felt condemned. Absolution is real: "I forgive you all your sins."
3. **The Bible in your own hands.** Luther wanted a plowboy to read Scripture. Today it's on your phone. Do you?
4. **Singing your faith.** Congregational hymns were a Reformation gift. "A Mighty Fortress" is still sung in every language.
5. **Ordinary work is holy.** Vocation: the farmer, the nurse, the mom, the mechanic serve God by serving their neighbor. No need to become a monk.
6. **Grace that goes outward.** Freed people are free to serve. Point to a food pantry, a youth group, or something concrete St. Paul's does in Troy.
7. **Not a new church, a return.** The Reformation was not about leaving; it was about getting back to Christ at the center.
