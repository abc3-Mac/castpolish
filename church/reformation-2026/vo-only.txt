A storm. A dirt road. A young law student, alone.

Lightning strikes so close it throws him to the ground. Martin Luther cries out: "Saint Anne, help me! I will become a monk!"

He kept that vow. He prayed, fasted, confessed for hours — and was still terrified. How could a sinner ever stand before a holy God?

Then he found it in Romans: "The righteous shall live by faith." Not earned. Not bought. Given — for Jesus' sake.

October 31, 1517. He nails ninety-five theses to a church door in Wittenberg. The printing press does the rest.

Soon ordinary people had a Bible in their own language, hymns they could sing, a catechism to teach at the kitchen table.
