## Accuracy notes for the pastor (so nobody has to fact-check in a comment thread)

- The thunderstorm was July 2, 1505, near Stotternheim, on the road back to Erfurt. Luther's vow to St. Anne is well attested; "thrown to the ground" follows his own later telling.
- Luther's "tower experience" with Romans 1:17 is dated variously (1515–1519). The script keeps it vague on purpose.
- The nailing of the Ninety-five Theses on Oct 31, 1517 is the traditional account (from Melanchthon). It is debated by some historians. If you prefer, change "nails" to "posts" — same syllable count, same timing.
- The German New Testament was translated at the Wartburg in about eleven weeks (1521–22) — a good fact for the Bible class or a follow-up post, not needed in the 90 s.
- "It was never about Luther" is Luther's own position. In 1522 he wrote: "I ask that men make no reference to my name; let them call themselves Christians, not Lutherans. What is Luther? After all, the teaching is not mine. Neither was I crucified for anyone." (A Sincere Admonition to All Christians, 1522; LW 45:70.)
- "God had already come down — in Jesus" is the script's plain-language gloss on Romans 1:17 read through Romans 3:21–26 and Philippians 2:6–8. It is not a quotation of Luther.
- **No generated imagery (decision 19 Oct 2026):** every frame is the pastor's own photo or footage (he took all of them; no third-party rights), so the post cannot be labeled AI-made. The Cranach portrait of Luther and the Stadtkirche altarpiece are public-domain works in his own photographs. The music is Luther's public-domain hymn, rendered locally. The painted Grok frames stay in `03-grok-clips/` as unused alternates.
- The cell segment is the **Erfurt Augustinian monastery** (pastor's photos): the cloister, then the altar in the monastery church. Luther entered this house in July 1505, made his profession there in 1506, and was ordained priest in Erfurt Cathedral on 3 April 1507 (first Mass in the monastery church, 2 May 1507). The caption says only "the altar where Luther lay prostrate before God", which holds for the profession rite and the first Mass without claiming the ordination took place at this altar.
- The **lightning** is a practical effect: white flashes over the Lutherstein photo graded dark and cold; no figure, no painting. The narration carries the fall.
- The **Lutherhaus** (the pastor's photo) now appears in the door/press montage: the Augustinian "Black Cloister" in Wittenberg where Luther lived as a monk from 1511 and, after 1525, with Katharina and the children. Luther's 1505–1511 monastery years were in Erfurt, so the caption calls it "the Augustinian monastery, Wittenberg" rather than the place of his vow.
- The opening photo is the **Lutherstein in the Dübener Heide** (granite boulder on the B2 between Bad Düben and Kemberg, the pastor's own photo). Tradition places Luther resting there in 1508 on the road from Erfurt to Wittenberg and preaching from it in 1519 after the Leipzig Disputation; it is inscribed "Eine feste Burg ist Gott" (added 1817). It is **not** the 1505 lightning site — that was Stotternheim, near Erfurt — so the caption says "on Luther's road from Erfurt" and the thunderstorm stays on the painted road.
- The pastor's segment was recorded 19 Oct 2026 (`04-broll/pastor-on-camera/IMG_0192-raw.mov`, take 2 at 38–57 s, cut to `pastor-take2.mp4`, 19.7 s); the closing line is `IMG_0193-raw.mov` take at 33.7–37.8 s, cut to `pastor-closing.mp4` (5.2 s), in front of the altar crucifix. He spoke it in his own words, about 19 s rather than the scripted 12; the script above now reads as recorded.
- The pastor segment and the invitation are the only parts that must be re-recorded if the times change. Keep those as separate takes.
